# Native 60 fps investigation

_2026-10-17. A summary checked against the current code, with earlier experimental results from
`docs/RESEARCH-60FPS.md` and `docs/HISTORY.md`. The experimental mode **already exists**; it was neither created nor
validated in gameplay in this audit._

## The loop and the 30 fps limit

The earlier mapping of `main` 1.0.0 found the loop in `f_70000231a0`, the update in `f_700001d1c0` and the end of the
frame in `f_7000029d30` (`docs/PROGRESS.md`). `runtime/nvn/objects.c` records the interval asked for by
`nvnWindowSetPresentInterval`; `nvnQueuePresentTexture` calls `fps60_present()` and `present_pace()`. The game asks for
interval 2 of a 60 Hz vsync; by default the runtime waits ~33.33 ms. The experimental option forces ~16.67 ms **only**
while the world updates; menu, battle and capture keep ~33.33 ms. `LARECOMP_FPS=60` alone only changes the pacing and
speeds up the logic (earlier measurement in `tools/repro.sh`).

The time step is not simply the vsync. The table `G` is at `main+0xf7eeb8` in Eevee 1.0.0, with `G[0]=16,666,667 ns`
and `G[1]=33,333,334 ns` (`main+0xf7eec0`). `f_7000039600` copies the value to step 1 of the timers object; the world
calls it on every update. `f_7000663660`/`f_7000664cd0`, `f_700067be50` and other consumers turn time into animation
progress. Step 0 stays at ~33.3 ms and some subsystems only test whether it is positive. The full map of readers and
addresses is in `docs/RESEARCH-60FPS.md`, "Time map".

The current 60 mode (`runtime/hle/fps60.c`) intercepts the accessor only during the world's update, offering 16.67 ms
to step 1 and restoring `G[1]` afterwards. `FIELD_UPDATE=0x70002f8bb0` tells the world apart from other scenes;
`ENCOUNTER_SCENE=0x700028ef70` and `BATTLE_START=0x700028bb60` mark capture/battle. `runtime/hle/wildbattle.c` marks
the wild battle through the alternative path. Pikachu 1.0.0's addresses are translated in `runtime/fnhook.c`; the
patches to the scene's counters are generated by `tools/patches.py`, never edited by hand in `generated/`.

## Frame-count dependencies

Animations that use step 1 can keep their speed in the world at 60. The capture and battle timelines use integer
counters; `tools/patches.py` changes three stores for the research into mode 2 (one tick every two updates).
Menus/Pokédex count frames too: an opening that took 8 frames at 30 took 4 in native 60 mode, per `docs/HISTORY.md` 47.
The current mode keeps their logic at 30 and presents an in-between frame; battle/capture stay at 30 without that frame
because in-game tests exposed interpolation defects (`HISTORY.md` 44–49). Physics, particles, scripts, camera, audio
and all battle timers have **not** been proven correct at 60 Hz.

## Alternatives

| Architecture | Possible gain | Dependencies and risk | Current view |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Simulation and rendering at 60 Hz | Every present gets a new real scene; lower latency. | Requires mapping and adjusting every counter and system that assumes 30 ticks/s. Mode 2 is research, not proof of correctness. | A good long-term goal, high regression risk. |
| B. Simulation at 30 Hz, rendering at 60 | Original speed, with the real pipeline redrawing the in-betweens. | Replaying NVN needs stable per-object identity, palettes, cuts and effects; GPU cost and latency. The earlier generic prototype was removed for ghosts, wrong menus and stretched models. | Feasible per scene with the game's markers; no validated general solution. |
| C. Hybrid (current) | The world at 60 without doubling the speed; other scenes keep known behaviour. | Transitions and interactions between systems need tests; the menus' quality depends on the interpolation. | The best balance of fidelity and risk **as things stand**, still experimental. |

An in-between frame in alternative B is a real draw of the pipeline with interpolated state; it is not optical-flow
generation. Even so, without identifying the same object in two frames, the image can be wrong. The mapping of
`f_70006237b0`/`f_7000641190` found stable object pointers in part of the world (`docs/RESEARCH-60FPS.md`), but it does
not cover every scene.

## Public patch and compatibility

[Fl4sh9174's public repository](https://github.com/Fl4sh9174/Switch-Emulator-Ultrawide-FPS-Mods) holds mod packs for
Eevee/Pikachu. The local decoding recorded in `docs/RESEARCH-60FPS.md` found, in the **1.0.2** patch, a write of
`16,666,667` to `main+0xf7eec0` and a branch at `main+0x5e78b8`. The first matches `G[1]` of the **1.0.0** dump; the
second **cannot be carried over by offset**: in 1.0.0 it is `blr x8`. The hypothesis that it changes the present
interval still depends on the 1.0.2 binary. Changing `G[1]` globally speeds up readers in other scenes. The 120 FPS
version writes ~10.7 ms, which is not even 1/120 s; there is no reason to apply it here.

[RT64](https://github.com/rt64/rt64/blob/main/include/rt64_extended_gbi.h) offers matrix-group commands with IDs and
interpolation options. This supports the direction of marking identity, but it does not show that the N64 protocol
can be copied to NVN. The local code and measurements take precedence over the claimed behaviour of outside mods.

## Prototype and validation state

The draw-replay prototype of 10/15 was removed; images and failures are in `docs/RESEARCH-60FPS.md`. The current
hybrid mode is off by default (`fps = 30`), turns on with `fps = 60` or `LARECOMP_FPS60=1`, and keeps 30 in the
critical scenes. `LARECOMP_FPS60=2` and `LARECOMP_FPS60_MENU_NATIVE=1` are research probes; a counter of 60 presents
does **not** attest correct gameplay.

In this audit, two scripted **Eevee 1.0.0** runs reached the world with a copied save. In 60 mode the log switched to
`world: 60 fps` at present 1342; over presents 1360–1449 the intervals had a mean of 16.540 ms, P95 18.152 ms, P99
18.585 ms, with no frame over 50 ms. The 30 mode had a mean of 33.334 ms over the same presents. The 60 mode's
screenshot showed the world, but **no comparison of gameplay position/time, animation, capture, battle or audio was
made**. The results only measure presentation in this short scene. Pikachu is untested too.

## Next technical steps

1. Instrument per-scene counters and clocks in Eevee/Pikachu; compare positions, animations, turns, capture and audio
   at the same game seconds at 30/60.
2. Show whether other threads read `G[1]` during the hook; replace the temporary global write with a safe local change
   if the race exists.
3. Extend the stable identity of the objects that record command lists. Replay one scene at a time, testing cuts,
   bones, shadows, UI, particles and objects appearing/disappearing.
4. Only move battle/capture/menus to native 60 Hz after fixing every relevant counter and validating gameplay, not
   just the visual duration.
